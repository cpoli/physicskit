Continuum Mechanics
-------------------

Linear continuum mechanics beyond flow. Fourier's heat equation smooths a
temperature field mode by mode. The explicit, Crank-Nicolson, and spectral
solvers show how stability and accuracy trade off. Sound in a pipe or a
room rings at a discrete set of standing-wave frequencies, recovered here
from a single pressure pulse. A pressurized thick-walled cylinder, solved
with plane-strain finite elements, reproduces Lamé's closed-form stresses.

In a pipe, reflections at the two ends allow only a discrete set of frequencies to persist: its standing modes. An end closed by a rigid cap reflects a pressure maximum. An open end, to a good approximation, fixes the pressure at ambient. As a result a pipe open at both ends (a flute) sounds all harmonics f_n = nc/2L, while a pipe closed at one end (a clarinet, roughly) sounds only the odd ones, f_n = (2n-1)c/4L, an octave lower.">
