    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Beta decay&#x27;s continuous electron spectrum was, on the assumption that it was a two-body process, a genuine puzzle: two-body decay kinematics forces the daughter to a single, sharply fixed momentum in the parent&#x27;s rest frame. Pauli (1930) proposed a third, undetected particle -- the neutrino -- making beta decay three-body instead, with the continuous spectrum simply reflecting three-body phase space. Fermi (1933-1934) built this into a full quantitative theory; Lattes, Occhialini, and Powell&#x27;s 1947 discovery of the charged pion supplied a clean, genuinely two-body confirmation of the other side of the same contrast: \pi^\pm\to\mu^\pm+\nu_\mu gives the muon one single, sharply fixed momentum, exactly as two-body kinematics demands. This example puts both cases side by side with two_body_decay_momentum and muon_decay_event.">

She found instead a pronounced asymmetry, dN/d\cos\theta\propto1+A\cos\theta with A\neq0 -- parity is violated in the weak interaction. This example reproduces exactly that signature using two_body_decay&#x27;s cos_theta parameter: sampling decay angles uniformly (the parity-conserving null hypothesis) gives a flat count-vs-\cos\theta histogram, while weighting the sample by 1+A\cos\theta for A\neq0 reproduces Wu&#x27;s asymmetric distribution directly.">
