Chaos Metrics
-------------

Examples illustrating the quantitative chaos-diagnostic tools in :mod:`physicskit.chaos.utils` and their
companion visualizers: trajectory-divergence-based and QR (Benettin)-based Lyapunov exponent
estimation, integrator energy drift, phase-space volume contraction, Poincare recurrence / Kac's
lemma, fractal (box-counting and correlation) dimension estimation, bifurcation diagrams for
periodically-forced continuous flows (see :doc:`/api/gallery/chaos/maps/plot_logistic_map` for the
discrete-map case), and Pomeau-Manneville intermittency near a tangent bifurcation.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Pomeau and Manneville (1980) identified a third route to chaos, distinct from period-doubling and quasi-periodicity: near a tangent (saddle-node) bifurcation, a trajectory spends increasingly long, apparently regular &quot;laminar&quot; episodes near the fixed point about to vanish, each interrupted by a short chaotic burst, with the mean laminar length diverging as a power law as the tangency is approached. LogisticMap&#x27;s period-three window is born at exactly such a tangent bifurcation, at r\approx3.8284; this example iterates the map at values of r just below that threshold and shows both halves of the phenomenon directly: the laminar-then-burst time series itself, and the mean laminar length growing as r approaches the tangency.">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="physicskit.chaos.visualizers.divergence.plot_lyapunov_divergence integrates two trajectories starting an infinitesimal distance \delta_0 apart and tracks \ln(\delta_t / \delta_0) over time, where \delta_t is their separation at time t. For chaotic motion this separation grows exponentially, \delta_t \sim \delta_0 e^{\lambda_{\max} t}, so a straight, positively-sloped line (its slope the largest Lyapunov exponent \lambda_{\max} &gt; 0) is the signature of chaos; a system that is not chaotic instead shows the separation shrinking (:math:`\lambda_{\max} &lt; 0`) as both trajectories relax toward the same attracting state. Two systems are contrasted below: the chaotic Lorenz attractor, \dot{x}=\sigma(y-x), \dot{y}=x(\rho-z)-y, \dot{z}=xy-\beta z; and the Duffing oscillator, \ddot{x}+\delta_D \dot{x}+\alpha x+\beta_D x^3=\gamma\cos(\omega t), with its cubic term and forcing switched off (\beta_D=\gamma=0, \alpha=1 &gt; 0), which reduces it to a plain damped linear oscillator, \ddot{x}+\delta_D \dot{x}+x=0: every trajectory relaxes to the same fixed point, so nearby trajectories converge rather than diverge.">
