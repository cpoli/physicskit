Vortex Dynamics
================

Concentrate all of a flow's vorticity into a handful of points and the
Navier-Stokes equations collapse to ordinary differential equations: each
point vortex simply drifts in the velocity field induced by every other one.
The examples below show how differently two vortices behave depending only
on the relative sign of their circulation -- a rigid, orbiting pair if the
signs match, a self-propelling dipole that runs off in a straight line if
they don't -- and then scale that same Biot-Savart mechanics up to a full
von Karman street, the staggered double row shed behind every bluff body
from a chimney to a violin string in the wind. Watch how the street holds
its staggered spacing intact at von Karman's stable ratio, in stark contrast
to the pairwise dynamics of the first example.
